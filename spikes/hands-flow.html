Option A
One case, many hands

Everyone sees the same case. Nobody re-types it.

Two hands write into the case. Two read out of it. It is the same file for all four, and it never leaves the middle.

Option B
One case, many hands

The cards you already have, with the case handed along.

Same four cards, same words. The chip is the case: it moves, the card it is sitting on lights, the wire between them marches for the hop.

ASSESSOR

Writes it at the house.

Photos, voice notes and old paperwork become the draft. They correct it and sign it.

LAB

A one-time link to upload what is missing and read the version they were sent. Nothing else.

Honest warning on this one: a left-to-right chain says the case is passed on, which is the thing the section is arguing against. Option A says it sits still and four hands reach it.

Option C
One case, many hands

Every hand makes the report bigger. The last one seals it.

The case is a chamber that does not move. What is inside it starts as an empty sheet and gains a part on every contribution — photos, core results, the I.S. 465 structure, the signature — until it stands as v1 and goes out.

The seal is the only step that cannot be taken back, so it gets the biggest hit: the report jumps past its final size and drops onto it. After that nothing moves — a frozen version should look finished, not busy.

Option D — replaces C
One case, many hands

Three people work the case. Two more are reached on paper.

Four sections, one per hand: the practice opens and assigns it, the assessor works the house, the homeowner supplies what is missing, the lab result comes back. The signature adds no fifth section — it signs off the four. The lab and the council sit outside the app, because in the app a lab is a field on a sample and the council has no account at all.

Two things this draws that the words alone do not: the sections exist before the content does, which is why nobody re-types into them, and the lab round trip is two pieces of paper crossing the line in opposite directions — an order out, a document back, recorded by the professional. The investigation engineer is left out: that is a second assessment on the same case and needs its own beat.
