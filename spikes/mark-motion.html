Four ways the mark can move.

The mark is the Ordnance Survey cut benchmark: an arrow pointing up at a fixed horizontal line, the surveyor's symbol for a height that has been measured and does not move. Any motion has to end with that line exactly where it started, or the mark argues against the product. Each candidate runs at 72 px, at the 26 px the landing page header uses, and in the wordmark lock-up. Click anywhere in a candidate to replay just that one.

Looping with a 1 s rest between runs. Reduced motion: every candidate holds its final frame.
A

Take the reading

The arrow rises from below and stops dead against the line; the line answers with one short amber widening, then holds. It reads as a reading being taken and then fixed — the staff comes up, the height is recorded, nothing moves after that.

Cost: 420 ms plus a 320 ms settle is the longest of the quiet options, and the rise is invisible at 16 px. Best used once on page load, not on every hover.

B

Cut in

The line is chiselled left to right, then the arrow is cut up to meet it, then the line holds amber for a beat. It is the same stroke-drawing motion the headline correction already uses on the landing page, so the page keeps one motion language instead of two.

Cost: drawing implies the mark is being made now, which is the opposite of a benchmark that was already there. It also needs a dash length per path, so redrawing the mark means recalculating them.

C

Shutter

Your camera idea, with the mark held still. A disc irises open from the arrow's point, covers the mark for about a tenth of a second, and irises away — and the line comes back amber for a beat, as if the exposure set it. The flash records the mark rather than disturbing it, which is what the app does: photograph the wall, fix the reading.

Cost: a black disc on the cream ground is a black disc, but on the dark app ground it has to become a light one to be visible at all, so the two grounds do not flash the same colour. At 26 px the disc is a 26 px blink and easy to mistake for a rendering glitch.

D

Turn, blitz, resume

Your idea as described: the line turns edge-on to face us, the blitz fires while it is turned, and it swings back to where it was. Shown so the cost is visible rather than argued.

Cost: two. The line collapsing to nothing is the exact thing the mark promises does not happen, so for about 300 ms the logo says the fixed height moved. And a perspective turn is carried entirely by the widths between 34 % and 54 % — at 26 px in the header those widths are under 3 px, so it reads as a flicker. 900 ms is also long enough to notice on every page load.
