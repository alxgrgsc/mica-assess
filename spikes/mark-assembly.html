The mark builds itself out of the wordmark.

One length of line, the same total length as the finished arrow. It breaks in two places and folds into the arrow. The arrow has no benchmark line yet, so it takes an amber stroke out of the wordmark, carries it back and sets it on top. Nothing is added and nothing is thrown away. Two versions below, differing in where the line starts and which letter it robs.

Underline start, the l becomes the centre line

The underline carries the two legs and the benchmark line, because the centre line is the l. The benchmark line breaks off the end, falls, and catches the middle of what is left by its own middle; it holds there balanced, then levels as the rest folds down under it, and lifts to its height. The assembly sinks clear of the word, and the l drops straight down into the centre without turning, because it is already the line it becomes.

This is the version where the amber is the centre line and everything else is ink, so the finished mark is not the one in docs/01-decisions.md. Both marks are in the strip at the bottom at 72 px and 26 px. Cost, unchanged: an underline has to span the word, so the line is drawn at about 1.6× and shrinks as it travels.

E

Standing start, robs the R

The line begins standing upright where the mark will end up, in the order leg, leg, stem, so that the top piece tips over onto its own twin and lands exactly on the point all three have to meet at. It folds into an arrow pointing at the wordmark, reaches across and takes the amber stem out of the R, then turns upright carrying it. There is no moment where one thing is swapped for another: the wordmark is sized so the R's stem is already 44 units long, and the mark's stroke is that stem's own width, so the amber line on the letter is the benchmark line from the first frame. It does not appear. It leaves.

Cost of the exact match: it sets three things you would otherwise choose. The wordmark's size, because its cap height has to equal what the line covers. Its weight, because its stem has to equal the mark's stroke. And the lock-up, because the benchmark line is longer than the mark is tall: if the line equals the cap height, the mark can only be about 0.7 of it, where logo-sheet.html draws it at about 1.09. The fitted numbers are in the header. Second cost, unchanged: a bare vertical line beside a wordmark is not a thing a page would have anyway, so the opening half second reads as something loading rather than resting.

The decision underneath both of them

Either way the stolen line is amber, because it came out of the wordmark. Leaving it amber makes the finished mark two-tone — ink arrow, amber line — which is a different mark from the single-colour one in docs/01-decisions.md. Settling it to ink keeps that mark and costs the payoff. The Line ends button switches both stages.

Both are landing-page openings, played once. The second half of each is a conversation between the mark and the wordmark, so neither can run where the mark appears alone: not the 26 px header mark, not the app top bar.
