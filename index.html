The I.S. 465 report, drafted before you leave the house.

Mica Assess is a reporting workspace for the assessment practices doing Defective Concrete Block work. Photos, voice notes and the homeowner's old documents go in on a tablet. The draft report comes out as a frozen PDF, edited by the professional who signs it. On site, with or without signal.

The problem

Thousands of houses. One report each, written up after the drive home.

Defective concrete block damage is a rural, one-house-at-a-time problem. The professional photographs the house on a phone, dictates or scribbles notes, and writes the report at a desk days later from whatever can be found. The backlog is in the write-up, not the visit. The tool that fixes it has to work in a field in Donegal with no signal, and still produce a document a council will take.

We built for the practice. Two of the seven boxes are professional reports: the initial BCA a homeowner commissions before applying, and the investigation the Agency directs later. Mica Assess keeps them as separate assessments in one case, because the second must not borrow the first's conclusions. Council and Agency get no login; they receive the PDF, as they do today. The case-scoped requests and handover packages exist to cut the missing-information loops, not to replace the council's portal.

How it works

Prepare, capture, autofill, edit, export.

Every step below runs in the prototype on synthetic cases. Where a step is simulated, it says so. The order is the order of a real job.

01

Open the case.

A practice administrator creates the case and allocates an assessor. The initial BCA and any later investigation are separate assessments inside it, so the second never inherits the first's conclusions. The work list keeps three things apart: how far the work is, whether it has synced, and what the scheme says about it.

02

Prepare from what already exists.

The professional uploads what the homeowner already has: the planning file, a previous engineer's report, old photos. An extraction job reads the readable ones and fills blank fields in sections A to D, each value labelled with its source document. An unreadable file is kept as an original and flagged, not silently dropped.

03

Capture on site, offline.

Locations are a list with thumbnails and inspection states: front elevation, left gable, kitchen, the roof void you could not get into and why. Each photo and dictated note gets its id on the tablet, is written to local storage first, and queues until there is signal. The same upload arriving twice is the same row.

04

Edit the report. Keep every human change.

The report follows the I.S. 465 Annex A structure, sections A to H, edited in place with a live preview. Rerunning extraction never overwrites something a person typed; the differing value is filed as an alternative to accept or ignore. A missing field shows a warning and does not block a draft.

05

Freeze a version.

Create version 1 and it is rendered to PDF by a headless browser, hashed, and stored with its snapshot. Edit and create version 2; version 1 is byte for byte what it was. There is no sign-off lock and no approval queue, because the declaration is the professional's, not the software's.

06

Share, request, hand over.

Invite the homeowner or a helper with a one-time link; they see an inbox, not the practice's cases. Request the documents the desk study needs, share one chosen version, revoke either. When the Agency directs an investigation, hand the case to that practice as a ZIP with a manifest and only the evidence you selected.

What is different

Built for the write-up, and for a tablet with no signal.

The tablet issues the ids.

A photo, a note or a document has an identity the moment it is captured, before any server has seen it. Every upload is an idempotent PUT with a key the server remembers, so the same thing arriving twice is the same row. Flight mode mid-visit breaks nothing. The queue drains when signal returns, and nothing is deleted locally until the server has it.

Drafts export with their warnings showing.

There is no approval queue and no lock. A missing field is a visible warning on the draft and in the PDF, not a closed door. Export never manufactures a declaration or implies the council accepted anything. The professional's declaration is a field they fill themselves.

Suggestions never overwrite a person.

Extraction and transcription produce typed suggestions. A blank field takes the suggestion and shows where it came from. A field someone typed keeps its value, and a differing suggestion is filed as an alternative. Rerun as often as you like; the human edits survive every time.

Versions are immutable, and can prove it.

Each report version freezes its snapshot and checks every photo's bytes against their stored hash, before and after rendering, then stores the PDF's own hash. Handover ZIPs carry a manifest with the same hashes. Download version 1 next year and it matches.

In the design
  • Document to fields. Reads the planning file, prior reports and lab sheets, and proposes values for the report's typed fields with the source they came from.
  • Voice to findings. A note dictated at the wall becomes a transcript and a proposed observation, with defect keys from the I.S. 465 Figure 2 list.
  • Retrieval, not training. No fine-tuning on assessor history. Every draft can point at the documents and recordings that shaped it, which matters in a grant process.
In this prototype
  • A labelled fixture. Every suggestion in the demo comes from a deterministic fake adapter reading a fixture file. The UI and the PDF mark it as simulated.
  • The live adapter exists and is unverified. A provider adapter sits behind the same interface and has not been run against real documents. No claim is made for its quality.
  • Nothing here is a formal assessment. Exports carry a draft marker. Lab results in the demo are synthetic and say so on the page.
